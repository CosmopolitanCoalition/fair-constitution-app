## Operator's batching question and one next bounded recommendation

**The chain must have an unambiguous append order; every jurisdiction does not inherently need its own durable commit.** Current code already batches up to 25 recipients within a jurisdiction and keeps its mint plus disbursement in one outer transaction. The global transaction lock protects the fresh chain head until commit. Different jurisdictions generally have different wallet sets, and role bumps can change amounts; they share an operation shape, not identical payment data.

A bounded group of jurisdictions can share the append ownership and commit while retaining each jurisdiction's recipient selection, authority/settings decisions, distinct mint/payment groups and receipts. Simply adding more lanes or removing the lock does not achieve that. A central/combined append path could also amortize head reads and inserts, but only if it preserves every group's validation, amounts and sequence-based hashing.

Measured commit time is **2.13 ms per paid scope**. Subtracting the two matched lock waits from the payment timer leaves approximately **9.67 ms** of non-waiting payment work; this includes some work before the first lock and is not a direct lock-held timer. An illustrative four-scope model with one equally costly commit and otherwise unchanged costs suggests roughly **20%** improvement from commit amortization alone. This is **not a forecast**: larger commits, savepoints, grouped writes, queue behavior and cache effects can change the result. Do not multiply throughput by the batch size or promise a large speedup from eliminating a two-millisecond commit alone.

Please make the next pass a **bounded Step 5 stipend batching prototype**, initially a small cap such as four scopes (lower if host limits require), and ship only if disposable tests demonstrate preserved semantics and less serialized work. Keep concurrency and ordinary non-simulation payment behavior unchanged. Preparation should happen before append ownership where it is independent of balances/head; preserve per-jurisdiction authority, settings, sample/order, role bumps, minted/treasury-draw behavior, amounts, posting groups and receipts. Retain the existing single ledger writer and its chain rules.

The main engineering prerequisite is recovery, not the loop syntax. `SimWorkerJob` currently claims one item, executes payment, and settles it separately. `StipendStage` explicitly tolerates a fresh disbursement when a paid item is re-handed. A batch must not multiply this gap. Require fenced ownership for every claimed item and either **atomic payment-plus-DONE settlement for the batch** or a durable, scope-specific payout identity that makes retry safe. A crash after payment commit but before queue acknowledgement must not pay the batch again. Failures must leave explicit, recoverable per-item outcomes; no silent loss of the other scopes. Keep halt latency, memory, claim count and transaction duration bounded; do not claim new batches after halt.

Use guarded disposable PostgreSQL to test two competing batches, mixed recipients/role amounts, required mint/credit/receipt failure rollback, ownership loss/reclaim, crash boundaries before and after durable commit, retries, partial/final batches, halt/drain, exact hashes/links, balances and receipt/metrics correspondence. Preserve the original default payment contract outside this Step 5 batch path. Record full batch/owned-commit time, total lock wait and post work, scopes and paid wallets; do not leave existing per-item timers ambiguously spanning a whole batch. A naive wrapper that adds savepoints without reducing measured cost should be reported as such, not sold as a speedup.

Return one completed tested handoff or a justified disposition. If this phase finishes before a safe implementation is ready, do not replay payments to create a benchmark. Retain the demonstrated index improvement and proceed to Phase 11. No batching code has been authored or applied on the demo.
