# K2 — Per-Screen Education Content, Wave 3 (the seven uncovered surfaces)

**Owner:** the Desk · **Authored:** 2026-09-10 · **status: authored**
The 2026-09-10 sweep found 7 of the 106 registered surfaces without an authored entry, so their
Learn flyout fell back to the module line (the operator saw it on the new place page: "the
learning information per surface seems to be missing"). This file closes the gap. Same template,
same voice rules, same cite discipline as `K2_CONTENT_SURFACES.md`: second person, present
tense, no jargon on first use, never promise a capability the code does not have.

---

### 1. `jurisdictions/place` — A place
**learn:** This is a place's own page — who it is, where it sits, what government it has, and how
you can take part. Anyone can read any place; living there is what lets you act there.

| # | do | detail | cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read where it sits | The chips at the top are its ancestors, root first. Each one is a link to that place's own page, so you can climb the tree without a map. | Art. I |
| 2 | Look at its government | A legislature exists once districts are drawn; it is seated once an election fills them. Executive and courts appear when this place creates them. "None yet" is a fact, not a fault. | Art. II §2 · Art. III · Art. IV |
| 3 | Open the map | The map viewer shows the boundary and the places inside it. It is one tool among several; the place page is the front door. | WF-JUR-01 |
| 4 | Take part | The public square, petitions and live rooms are open to everyone. Filing a form here needs residency; reading never does. | Art. I; F-IND-003 |

> **The why:** a role gates actions, never pages. Every place is public because the record of
> government is public — you can watch any jurisdiction govern itself before you ever live there.

### 2. `system/atlas` — The Atlas
**learn:** The Atlas is the live, public heartbeat of the whole game: every node, every place that
has woken up, and the running numbers. It shows the world; it never touches anyone's rights.

| # | do | detail | cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read the counters | Places founded, legislatures seated, elections under way — each figure is computed from the record, not typed in by anyone. | Art. II §2 |
| 2 | Find a place on the map | Appearing on the map is opt-in and approximate. A dot is a place's choice to be seen, never a person's location. | Art. I; F-IND-005 |
| 3 | Watch reach and growth | These are display-only. Nothing here is a lever: no threshold on the Atlas unlocks or blocks a vote, a candidacy, or a seat. | Art. I |

> **The why:** the constitution makes the record public and the person private. The Atlas is the
> public half rendered as a picture.

### 3. `social/legitimacy` — Reach
**learn:** Reach is a gauge of how much of a place's population has confirmed residency and taken
part. It tells you how representative a jurisdiction's government is today; it decides nothing.

| # | do | detail | cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read the gauge | Confirmed residents over measured population. A low figure means few people have shown up yet, not that the government is invalid. | Art. I; F-IND-006 |
| 2 | Compare places | Every place reads the same way, at every level, so a neighbourhood and a country are comparable at a glance. | Art. V §1 |
| 3 | Do not look for a threshold | There is none. Reach never gates an election, a seat, or a right; the only requirement anywhere is residency. | Art. I |

> **The why:** legitimacy grows as people arrive. The gauge is honest about where a place stands
> so that nobody has to trust a claim.

### 4. `build/progress` — Building the world
**learn:** This page shows the world being founded: places waking up, districts drawn, chambers
seated. It is a window on the machine, and nothing on it asks anything of you.

| # | do | detail | cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read the ladder | Each step is a stage every place passes through: boundary loaded, enough people, districts drawn, chamber seated. | CLK-06; Art. II §2 |
| 2 | Follow one place | Pick a place and watch its own row move. When its legislature is seated its place page shows the chamber. | WF-JUR-01 |
| 3 | Leave it running | The build is clocked and resumable. If it pauses it picks up where it stopped; no official restarts it by hand. | CLK-06 |

> **The why:** governments here are founded by rule, not by decree. Watching the rule run is how
> you know nobody chose who gets a legislature.

### 5. `economy/resident-agreements` — Resident agreements
**learn:** A resident agreement is a contract between people — two of you or many — written in
clauses you can redline together. You may agree to almost anything; you may never sign away a
right.

| # | do | detail | cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Start an agreement or open one you were named in | Name the parties and write the clauses in plain words. Every party sees the same text. | F-IND-020 |
| 2 | Redline a clause | Propose a change to one clause; the others accept or counter. The history of every redline stays on the record. | F-IND-020 |
| 3 | Sign | An agreement binds when every party has signed the same version. A clause that would waive a constitutional right is refused before signing, not argued about after. | F-IND-020 · Art. I |

> **The why:** freedom to contract is real here, and so is its one limit: the constitution's rights
> are not yours to trade, even willingly.

### 6. `shared/coverage` — Coverage
**learn:** Coverage shows which parts of the app are built, which are partly built, and which are
still planned. It is the honest map of the software itself.

| # | do | detail | cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read the colours | Green is live and reachable. Anything else is Planned: the page or the capability is not there yet, and the app says so instead of pretending. | R-01 |
| 2 | Open a row | Each row names the route and the surface behind it, so you can go straight to what is live. | R-01 |
| 3 | Report a gap | If something green does not work for you, Report an issue from any Learn drawer files it with the surface id attached. | R-01 |

> **The why:** the operator's rule is that a first walk never lands on a placeholder. Coverage is
> how that promise is checked in the open.

### 7. `shared/coverage-ops` — Coverage matrix
**learn:** The coverage matrix is the release checklist: every registry row, tour stop and surface
must resolve to a real page before a build is handed over.

| # | do | detail | cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read the matrix | Rows are registry entries; columns are the checks (route exists, page renders, surface registered, learn copy present). | R-01 |
| 2 | Chase the red | A red cell is a broken promise somewhere in the registry. The row tells you which file to fix. | R-01 |
| 3 | Hand over on green | All green is what "done" means for a release; a partly green matrix ships as a known gap, never as done. | R-01 |

> **The why:** the app's own honesty is checked the same way its governments are — against a
> record anyone can read.
